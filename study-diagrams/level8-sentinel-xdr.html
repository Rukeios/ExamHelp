Level 8: Microsoft Sentinel and Defender XDR

Two products, one security team. Sentinel collects everything and automates the response; Defender XDR guards email, devices, identities and apps and joins their alerts into one incident.

Events➜Alerts➜Incidents
Raw activity → something a rule flagged → related alerts grouped together. An incident is a collection of correlated alerts.
Workbooks = LOOK (dashboards). Playbooks = ACT (automated response on Azure Logic Apps).
MICROSOFT SENTINEL: cloud-native SIEM + SOAR, no servers to run

1. Collect

Data connectors bring in logs from users, devices, apps and infrastructure, on-premises and in any cloud.

➜

2. Detect

Analytics rules search the data and raise alerts, which are grouped into incidents.

➜

3. Investigate

Analysts dig into incidents. Hunting means searching for threats before any alert fires.

➜

4. Respond

Automation rules and playbooks act on incidents. Playbooks are built on Azure Logic Apps.

Workbooks

Dashboards for viewing data, with built-in templates (Azure Monitor workbooks) for quick insights.

MICROSOFT DEFENDER XDR: each Defender guards one area; the Defender portal joins related alerts into one incident

Defender for Office 365

Email and collaboration: phishing, malicious links and attachments.

Defender for Endpoint

Devices: laptops, phones, servers. Includes endpoint detection and response (EDR).

Defender for Cloud Apps

A CASB: visibility and control over cloud apps. Finds unapproved apps (shadow IT).

Defender for Identity

Sensors on on-prem domain controllers. Spots compromised identities and attackers moving sideways.

Also in the Defender portal

  • Vulnerability Management: finds weaknesses and ranks them by risk
  • Threat intelligence: threat analytics reports and intel profiles of known attackers
  • One portal for incidents, hunting, Sentinel and Defender for Cloud

Not the same as Level 7: Defender for Cloud protects Azure resources. Defender for Cloud Apps watches SaaS apps people use.
