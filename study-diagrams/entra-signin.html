Entra ID: what happens when someone signs in

Levels 3–5. Read left to right: who signs in → prove who you are → Conditional Access decides → what you're allowed to do. Everything gets logged.

0. WHO signs in

Identity types

  • Users: employees
  • External identities: guests from other companies (B2B)
  • Devices: registered or joined laptops and phones
  • Workload identities: apps and services. Managed identities, service principals
  • Groups: assign access to many at once. Dynamic groups add and remove members by rules (e.g. department = Sales)

On-premises: AD DS

The old in-house directory. Holds objects: users, computers, printers, servers. Kerberos, group policy.

Entra Connect syncs it up to Entra ID (hybrid identity).

Modern auth and SaaS apps live in Entra ID, not AD DS.

➜
1. AUTHENTICATION: prove who you are

Password + MFA

MFA = two or more of: something you know (password, PIN), have (phone, key), are (face, fingerprint).

Second factors: Authenticator push with number matching (stops MFA fatigue), SMS, voice, OATH tokens.

Password protection: blocks banned and weak passwords. SSPR: users reset their own password; writeback sends it to on-prem AD.

Passwordless: no password at all

  • Windows Hello for Business: PIN, face or fingerprint. The key is bound to that one device. It doesn't sync to other devices and doesn't use the Authenticator app.
  • Microsoft Authenticator: phone sign-in
  • FIDO2 security keys / passkeys
  • Certificate-based authentication

NOT passwordless: SMS, voice calls, OATH software/hardware tokens. Those are MFA second factors.

Security defaults

Free. One switch turns on MFA for everyone and blocks legacy sign-ins. Use it if you don't have Conditional Access.

➜
2. CONDITIONAL ACCESS: if… then… P1

IF: signals

  • User or group
  • Location / IP address
  • Device: is it compliant (Intune)?
  • Which app
  • Sign-in risk and user risk from ID Protection

THEN: decision

Block
Allow
Allow if…

"Allow if" = require MFA, a compliant device, accepting terms of use (acceptance is recorded), or a password change.

ID Protection P2

Detects risky sign-ins (leaked credentials, impossible travel, anonymous IPs) and risky users. Feeds the risk level into Conditional Access.

Conditional Access is signal → decision → enforcement. Location and device are signals; "require MFA" is a decision.
➜
3. AUTHORIZATION: what you can do

Access granted

Entra issues a token. Single sign-on: sign in once, reach many apps.

Roles (RBAC)

Give the least privilege that does the job. Built-in or custom roles.

PIM: Privileged Identity Management P2

Just-in-time admin rights: time-limited, can require approval and MFA, every activation is logged.

Identity governance

  • Access reviews: does this person still need this?
  • Entitlement management: access packages people request
  • Lifecycle: joiners, movers, leavers. Dynamic groups automate the access lifecycle
4. AUDITING: sign-in logs and audit logs record who did what, when. Tracking what a user accessed = auditing.
